A central concept in GRANERGIZE is the \emph{Data Room}. Data Rooms are demarcated virtual areas with clearly defined access conditions, controlled by a gatekeeper. Only approved participants are permitted to enter, based on explicit authorization. Within a Data Room, stakeholders can negotiate and formalize data sharing agreements, ensuring that trust and consent are established before any data is exchanged.

We deliberately chose the term Data Room, as these spaces are conceptualized as protected and restricted areas. Access is managed by a gatekeeper -- typically the data owner -- who determines which participants may enter and under what conditions. The Data Room thus provides a secure environment for stakeholders to discover available data, specify their own data requirements, and negotiate mutually acceptable terms for data exchange. If the data offered meets a participant's needs, they can submit their own data offers in return and, if there is mutual consent, agree to exchange data. Otherwise, participants can leave the Data Room without further interaction.

To illustrate this, consider the following scenario: the facility manager (FM) acts as the owner of a Data Room. Various tenants and brokers are granted access to this room to view descriptions of the data the FM provides, specifying the data's granularity and periodicity. If the data description matches their requirements, they can offer their own data descriptions and, upon agreement, exchange data.

Energy consumption data in logistics properties is relevant for a wide range of stakeholders. While users, owners, and FMs are primarily interested in specific consumption data for reporting and calculation purposes, developers, financiers, brokers, energy suppliers, and benchmark service providers (BSP) rely on data for property comparisons, accurate energy reporting, and consumption optimization.

To capture these interests and requirements, the project team surveyed each stakeholder group about the implications of energy consumption data for their daily business, the associated pains and gains, and any data requirements they might have from other stakeholders. \Cref{tbl:facroom} shows an example Data Room owned by a FM, who exchanges consumption data with brokers (e.g., for energy performance certificates) and tenants (e.g., detailed consumption breakdowns including blind and peak loads), while receiving building specifics and tenant-side consumption data in return. \Cref{tbl:invroom} illustrates a second Data Room in which an investor shares building specifics and consumption data with a BSP, who in turn computes and shares back aggregated consumption per~m\textsuperscript{2}.
